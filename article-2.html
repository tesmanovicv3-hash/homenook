Refresh Your Kitchen Without Renovating — 7 Cheap Swaps

KITCHEN · BUDGET REFRESH

A full kitchen renovation is expensive and disruptive, but most of what makes a kitchen feel dated isn't the cabinets themselves — it's the small details layered on top of them. Here are seven changes that cost a fraction of a remodel but change how the whole room reads.

Seven changes worth making first

  1. Swap the cabinet hardware. Old brass or plastic knobs are one of the fastest ways to date a kitchen. New handles in a matte black, brushed brass, or aged bronze finish change the whole feel in an afternoon.
  2. Add a peel-and-stick or paintable backsplash. A backsplash update behind the stove and sink draws the eye away from tired countertops.
  3. Paint just the lower cabinets. Painting only the base cabinets a deep color while leaving the uppers light is a classic way to modernize a kitchen without repainting everything.
  4. Replace one light fixture. A single pendant light over the sink or island often has more visual impact than any other single change in the room.
  5. Remove upper cabinet doors on one section. Open shelving on just one or two cabinets breaks up a wall of identical doors and creates a spot to display dishes or plants.
  6. Add a runner rug. A patterned runner in front of the sink adds color and texture at floor level, which is easy to overlook.
  7. Update the faucet. A faucet in the same finish as your new hardware pulls the whole look together for a relatively small cost.

A simple order to tackle them in

Start with hardware and lighting — they're the cheapest and fastest, and they immediately change how the space feels while you decide if you want to take on paint or a backsplash next. Save structural changes, like removing cabinet doors, for last, since they're the hardest to reverse.
