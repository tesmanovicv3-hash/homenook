Where to Place the Bed in a Small Bedroom

BEDROOM · SMALL SPACE LAYOUT

In a small bedroom, bed placement decides almost everything else — how much floor space feels open, whether you can fit storage, and how easy the room is to move around in. A few simple rules solve most small-bedroom layout problems before you buy a single piece of furniture.

Start with the door and the window, not the bed

Before deciding where the bed goes, map out where the door swings and where the window sits. The bed should never block a door's swing or sit directly in front of a window if it can be avoided — both waste the room's best natural light and airflow.

Push it into a corner

In rooms under about 110 square feet, placing the bed with one long side against a wall and the headboard in a corner is usually the most efficient option. It frees up an entire side of the room for a dresser, desk, or simply walking space, instead of splitting the floor in half.

Float it only if the room allows

Centering the bed on a wall, with equal space on both sides, looks the most "finished" in photos, but it only works if the room is wide enough to leave at least 24–30 inches of walking space on each side. In a genuinely small room, this arrangement often eats the space you need for a closet door or dresser.

Use the height of the room, not just the floor

  • Wall-mounted nightstands or floating shelves free up floor space that legged nightstands take up.
  • A bed with built-in drawers replaces a dresser entirely in very tight rooms.
  • Curtains instead of blinds can make a low ceiling feel taller if hung close to the ceiling line rather than just above the window frame.

Skip the full bed frame if space is very tight

In the smallest rooms, a low platform frame or even a mattress on a simple base reads as more spacious than a tall frame with a large headboard, simply because it keeps the sightline across the room open.
